WhoGaveWhat for iPhone

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 16, 2026

WhoGaveWhat (shown as “Who Gave” on the Home Screen) is an app for remembering the gifts you give and receive. It is made by Danis Ziganshin, who is responsible for your information as described here. This policy explains what the app does with your information.

  • Your gifts, people, photos and settings stay on your iPhone, except gift names you choose to send for cloud icon suggestions.
  • There are no accounts, ads, third-party analytics or tracking.
  • Cloud icon suggestions are optional and off until you allow them.

Information stored on your device

The people, gifts, amounts, dates, occasions and photos you add are stored only in the app’s storage on your iPhone. Apart from gift names you choose to send for cloud icon suggestions (below), they are not uploaded to any server, and I have no access to them. They are included in backups of your iPhone that you make with iCloud or a computer, under your backup settings. Deleting the app deletes this information from your iPhone.

When you add a photo of a person, you pick it with the system photo picker. The app receives only the photo you choose and keeps a copy on your device. It cannot see the rest of your photo library.

Without cloud icon suggestions, the app picks gift icons on your device, using Apple Intelligence where it is available and turned on, or a built-in word list.

Cloud icon suggestions (optional)

WhoGaveWhat can suggest an icon for a gift using the Jev model from TypeSafe AI. This feature is off until you allow it in the Add Gift screen or turn on Settings → Cloud icon suggestions. You can turn it off there at any time, and the app then picks icons on your device again.

What is sent

When the feature is on, the app sends a request when you allow the feature or pause while typing a gift name. Each request contains:

  • the gift name you typed, up to 80 characters;
  • a random identifier created for this feature and used only to limit how many requests one installation can make. It is not derived from your device or Apple account, and a new one is created whenever you turn the feature off;
  • standard network information that every internet request carries, such as your IP address and the app and iOS versions.

The app never sends people, contacts, dates, amounts, occasions or photos.

Gift names can contain personal information. If you would rather not share a name, leave the feature off or choose the icon yourself.

Where it goes

The request goes to the WhoGaveWhat server, which I run on Cloudflare Workers. The server forwards only the gift name to TypeSafe AI, which chooses an icon and returns it.

  • The WhoGaveWhat server does not log gift names, identifiers or IP addresses, or store them in readable form. To limit requests, it counts them under codes made from the identifier and IP address with a random key that changes every day. Each key, and the counts made with it, are deleted within three days. To avoid repeating requests, it keeps each chosen icon under a code made from the gift name, not linked to you, and deletes it after 30 days.
  • Cloudflare runs the server and so processes each request, including the gift name, the identifier and network information, to deliver and protect the service, under the Cloudflare Privacy Policy.
  • TypeSafe AI receives only the gift name and processes it to choose an icon, under its Master Customer Agreement and Privacy Policy. TypeSafe states that it does not train or fine-tune AI models on request data. Under those terms it may keep request data, and logs and statistics derived from it, without a fixed time limit, to provide and improve its service, prevent abuse and meet legal obligations, and may share it with its own service providers.

Cloudflare and TypeSafe may process requests outside your country, including in the United States, under the terms above.

Information from Apple

If you choose to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone’s settings, Apple may give me crash reports and aggregated usage statistics that do not identify you. If you test a TestFlight build, Apple also gives me the crash reports and feedback you choose to send.

What I do not do

  • I do not sell your information or share it for advertising.
  • I do not track you across apps or websites.
  • I do not use your information to train AI models.

Your choices and rights

Cloud icon suggestions rely on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time by turning the feature off in Settings. Requests already sent are not affected.

  • Edit or delete any person or gift in the app, or delete the app to remove everything from your iPhone.
  • Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access or delete your personal information, or to object to how it is used. Because the WhoGaveWhat server keeps nothing that identifies you, and deletes its request counts within three days, there is usually nothing to look up, but email me and I will help, including forwarding your request to TypeSafe.
  • You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

WhoGaveWhat is not directed at children under 13, and I do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Changes to this policy

If the app’s handling of information changes, I will update this page and the date above before the change takes effect.
